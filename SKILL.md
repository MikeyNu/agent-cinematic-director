# Cinematic Director

Turn the brief into observable choices, then execute and inspect them. Give every image a purpose and a primary point of attention. For motion, define the temporal change and reason to end each shot; for a still, apply the spatial, color and delivery checks. Treat cinematic quality as coherent visual storytelling appropriate to the intended style. Preserve intentional animation, clean commercial imagery, documentary restraint, and other styles without forcing photorealism or a film preset onto them.

## Start with the smallest useful workflow

1. Identify the requested action: **plan, generate, edit, inspect, or deliver**. Continue work already authorized; carry existing constraints and spending authority forward. Research and skill assessment do not themselves authorize paid media generation.
2. Extract audience, message/emotional beat, medium, duration, aspect ratios, delivery size/frame rate, sound, references, exact text/branding, invariants, and available tools. Infer reversible creative details and state assumptions. Ask only for a missing decision that materially blocks the result.
3. Read the relevant reference below, not the whole library. Read the companion handoff before using another skill. Inspect the actual source media and current tool capabilities before committing a production plan.
4. Propose one coherent treatment and a compact shot list. For a one-shot correction, identify the defect and local repair directly. For a sequence, allocate time to comprehension, action, reaction, and a readable landing.
5. Execute the least costly useful proof: framing board, blocked camera move, style frame, animatic, or one representative shot. Use approved references and supported controls. Advance once the proof resolves the actual uncertainty.
6. Review at normal speed and delivery size, then inspect targeted native frames. Repair the smallest failing unit, check the surrounding sequence, and stop when the agreed criteria are met or the bounded repair route needs to change.

## Make direction executable

Define each shot with these decisions. Omit irrelevant fields rather than producing a long generic checklist.

| Decision | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Narrative function | What changes in the viewer's understanding or feeling? |
| Staging and attention | Primary subject, its position, foreground/background, blocking, gaze and intended reading order. |
| Camera and optics | Shot size, viewpoint/height, movement from opening to landing, lens intent with sensor assumption if numeric, focus target and change. |
| Light and color | Motivated key/fill/practicals, contrast and palette roles, protected skin/product colors, output context. |
| Time and motion | Subject action, camera action, secondary action, acceleration/settle, meaningful start/end and edit handles. |
| Continuity | Identity, geometry, wardrobe, prop contact, lighting, screen direction, eyelines and audio that must persist. |
| Execution | Actual tool/model/mode, reference roles, supported hard controls, soft prompt intent, deterministic post work. |
| Acceptance | Observable success, hard failures, inspection method and repair limit within existing authority. |

Separate **hard controls**, **prompted intent**, and **postproduction requirements**. A prose timestamp is not an animation keyframe. An AI lens label does not verify simulated optics. A last-frame reference does not guarantee continuity. Use a deterministic camera scene, animation, tracked composite, or edit when frame accuracy, exact geometry, typography, or logos matter.

Choose viewpoint before focal length. At a fixed viewpoint, focal length primarily changes field of view; moving the camera changes perspective. Describe subject/background relationships before naming millimeters. Specify a sensor/filmback assumption for real optical calculations. Preserve sufficient depth of field for the required action and text. Use focus to transfer attention, not to conceal a failed subject.

Name one principal camera action and a clear subject event as the starting point. Add complexity only when it serves the beat and the tool can sustain it. Distinguish pan/tilt rotation, dolly/truck translation, zoom, and rack focus. For complicated action, use coverage and editing or a controlled scene instead of accumulating contradictory prompt verbs.

Use composition as attention design. Inspect a small thumbnail: does the intended subject win? Adjust staging, silhouette, contrast, color difference, gaze, motion and negative space. Apply thirds or golden-ratio guides only when they improve this shot. Verify every requested aspect ratio separately and reserve actual interface/caption safe areas supplied by the destination.

## Direct motion, time and finish

Apply the twelve animation principles selectively to cameras, props, transitions, typography and environments as well as characters. Preserve the physical meaning of the subject: elastic timing may help an icon, while rubbery product geometry may violate the brief. Establish pose, path and timing before secondary movement or decorative effects.

Motivate shake through an operator, impact, vibration or intentional style. Define its trigger, amplitude relative to frame size, frequency, directional bias and decay. Check the image after stabilization/cropping and after compression. Let a premium tabletop shot remain stable when stability serves it.

Cut when a beat, information change, gaze, action, sound or deliberate rhythmic contrast motivates it. Give the viewer time to identify, interpret and read. Evaluate in real-time playback with the actual dialogue, captions and sound design; a contact sheet cannot validate pacing or sync. Treat numeric shot lengths and shutter conventions as starting hypotheses, not universal rules.

Establish source and output color handling before the look. Keep correction, shot matching, creative grading and delivery transforms conceptually separate, even when the application combines them. Do not guess that generated media is camera log or apply a camera LUT to unknown material. Choose contrast, hue separation and saturation for the story and subject, protecting required skin and brand appearance.

Add grain, halation, bloom, sharpening or softness only for a stated material/period/style purpose. Judge them at final scale and in the delivered encode. Do not use noise, blur, darkness, motion blur or fast cuts to disguise geometry, identity or temporal failures.

## Work with tools and companion skills

Keep creative decisions here; let the installed execution skill own its current API syntax, model selection, credentials, quoting, job recovery and spending controls. Read a user-supplied skill before invoking it. If it is unavailable, report that specific limitation and continue with the supported local workflow. Do not invent commands or claim a handoff occurred.

- **Higgsfield Director (`higgsfield-director`):** pass shot cards, reference roles, invariants and acceptance criteria; discover current model controls and compile the smallest supported request. Preserve existing authorization and native tool gates. Recover an uncertain job before any resubmission.
- **AI Detector (`agent-ai-detector`):** request a quality review with original media, intended style, lineage and time/region evidence. Keep quality and provenance separate. Use its actual report schema if available; accept `passes_visual_qa`, `needs_revision`, or `not_assessed` without inventing a probability of authenticity.
- **ElevenLabs Speech (`elevenlabs-speech`):** pass performance intent, spoken text, pronunciation and available editorial time. Review actual speech before locking cuts or lip sync.
- **Cinematic Scroll Director (`cinematic-scroll-director`):** pass approved visual beats, camera/continuity requirements, reading holds, mobile composition and a reduced-motion alternative; leave scroll implementation and presentation checks to that skill.

For exact editorial plans, copy [the example](assets/shot-plan.example.json), adapt it using the [contract](references/shot-contract.md), and run:

```bash
python3 <skill-path>/scripts/check_plan.py path/to/shot-plan.json
```

Treat the result as timeline structure validation only. It cannot assess aesthetics, actual footage, current model support, cost authority, or audience comprehension.

## Inspect, repair and deliver

Review the whole sequence once at normal speed for story, attention, rhythm and continuity. Then examine faces, hands, contacts, reflections, repeated structures, typography, occlusions, joins and endpoint frames at native scale. Inspect consecutive frames around a defect using actual timestamps when available. Recheck the exported delivery file and its audio.

Record each finding as **time/region, observed defect, severity, plausible cause, targeted fix, preserved invariants, and acceptance check**. Distinguish an observed fact from a hypothesis. Do not call unviewed footage clean or infer temporal stability from isolated stills. Delegate an independent pass when practical.

Fix critical geometry/identity/action failures before cosmetic finishing. Try one targeted correction, recheck its neighbors, and compare against the baseline. After two unsuccessful attempts at the same defect, change the method or explain the remaining constraint; never silently exceed a lower attempt/budget cap. Preserve accepted shots and source provenance throughout. A quality pass does not change known synthetic origin.

Deliver the requested artifact plus a concise decision record: treatment, executed tools/settings, important shot/edit/color choices, actual checks, unresolved limitations and file locations. Distinguish planned, generated, inspected and accepted states. Include a shot list or grading rationale only when it helps the next production step. Refresh only the source or capability that is uncertain; do not repeat broad research for routine work.
